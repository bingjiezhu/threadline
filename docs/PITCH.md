# Threadline — English pitch and launch copy

## Positioning

**Keep the context. Find the right moment.**
A private relationship workspace for consultants and service-company owners who handle their own client development.

## 30-second introduction

The next useful client conversation may already be hidden in the last one. A client asked for something you could not deliver then. Months later, your capabilities changed—but that conversation is buried in your notes. Threadline connects the original requirement with the new evidence, keeps the remaining unknowns visible, and helps you decide who to contact and what to ask. Your records persist. Your judgement stays yours.

## 90-second pitch

Imagine a small software consultancy. Its owner handles discovery, client relationships, and delivery.

In May, a client says, “We need this inside our own environment. Come back when you have a relevant example.” In October, the consultancy completes exactly the kind of delivery that could reopen the discussion. But the earlier conversation is buried in meeting notes, and the contact may have changed roles.

Threadline helps the owner connect those moments.

It keeps people, projects, original conversations, and reviewed evidence together. When a later confirmed capability matches an earlier project requirement, it surfaces a reason to take another look. It shows both quotations, preserves what is still unknown, and helps prepare a careful message. It never pretends that a technical match proves a budget, buying intent, or a sale.

The owner decides what to approve, whom to contact, and what actually happened. Those results become part of the next conversation's context.

The current release is a usable, self-hosted private workspace. It starts empty, works with your own records, and does not need an AI key for its core workflow. Optional AI assists with extraction and drafting; it does not own your decisions.

We are testing a $49-per-owner guided pilot with small AI and software implementation firms. The next proof is repeated use and a genuine second payment—not the number of contacts imported.

Threadline. Keep the context. Find the right moment.

## 3-minute investor discussion

Start with the people whose client-development process lives largely in the owner's memory: small consulting and implementation firms. They already create the raw material of a valuable relationship system every week—discovery notes, client objections, delivery results, and promises. The expensive work is maintaining the connections among those pieces over time.

A client requirement from six months ago and a new delivery capability might create a legitimate reason to reconnect. A new blocker might instead mean the right decision is to wait. The owner needs both kinds of answer, with the original context intact.

Threadline's initial workflow is deliberately narrow. It stores the original source, proposes or records evidence, asks the owner to approve its interpretation, matches current conditions, and prepares the next step for human review. Actual contact and business outcomes are separate records; a copied draft is never reported as a sale.

The shipped self-hosted release handles real accounts and arbitrary new records. It includes contact and project management, notes, quotations, explicit approval, local matching, editable drafts, tasks, history, data export and deletion. Optional OpenAI extraction and drafting have a real provider path; no live-provider performance claim is made before evaluation.

This is not an empty category. Relationship tools, AI CRM systems, and customer-memory products already offer adjacent capabilities. Attio even demonstrates outreach based on past feature requests. Our opportunity must come from doing a defined owner's workflow with lower total setup and maintenance effort—not from claiming we invented memory or sourced answers.

We propose to test that with ten independent companies at a clear pilot price, tracking submitted context, owner effort, helpful decisions, actual next steps, and renewal. Six second payments out of ten would justify another iteration; it would not prove product-market fit. If the workflow requires continuous founder assistance, or a mature competitor does it better at comparable cost, we should change the approach.

If the owner workflow works, expansion can follow the work: one authorized connector to make capture natural, then explicitly shared project handoff so a colleague can pick up where the owner left off. Customer data remains portable and models remain replaceable. The future asset is an increasingly reliable, authorized workflow and a history of corrected decisions—not a pile of inaccessible private data.

Today we have a usable product and a disciplined path to validation. What we need next is evidence from real use and payment. The fundraising amount and runway should be derived from the actual team, operating costs and milestones; none is fabricated in this deck.

## Speaker discipline

Use “implemented” only for the current release features. Say “optional adapter, not live-tested here” for external AI. Say “proposed pilot” for pricing and “not yet measured” for acquisition/retention. All companies in the walkthrough are fictional. Explain self-hosting, privacy, limitations, and manual contact clearly. Never substitute prospect inquiries, code downloads or a model demo for paying customers.
