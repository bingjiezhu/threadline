# Warm Start commercial hypothesis

## User, buyer, job

User: a newly hired engineer or solutions professional who has a concrete first task but cannot reliably reconstruct which source is current or who owns the next step. Buyer hypothesis: the person's manager or a people-operations lead paying for a controlled onboarding pilot. The present application is private and single-owner; do not sell a shared company ACL or manager dashboard that does not exist.

The original independent-consultant/owner $49 hypothesis is preserved in historical research. Warm Start is a new entry workflow, not evidence that the earlier segment or price was validated. Keep cohorts and results separate.

## Why this is worth testing

The official Workday challenge describes hidden knowledge, tools, people and expectations as a new-hire problem. That establishes sponsor relevance, not market demand. The product aims to reduce reconstructing context and stale-owner mistakes on a real first task. Test against the existing handbook + Slack search + human buddy process, and reasonably configured knowledge assistants. Do not claim inventing memory or enterprise search.

## Initial experiment

Recruit five managers from software/implementation teams and two consenting new hires per manager. Begin with one permitted goal and a handful of authorized documents. Observe setup, source correction, task approval and whether a human verifies the defined outcome. Record a baseline workflow for a comparable task; do not claim causal improvement from an anecdote or different task difficulty.

Offer an explicitly negotiated, short paid guided pilot only after the workflow works on their data. A possible $199 four-week small-cohort offer is a *price hypothesis*, including clearly bounded setup/support—not a price inside the app or proven willingness to pay. Host protection, agreements, payment and account operations must exist before charging. No actual outreach or payment was executed.

Record invited, qualified, offered, accepted, paid, refunded and renewed counts. Track manager setup minutes, hire correction minutes, unresolved cases, completed outcomes confirmed by a person, and ongoing support cost. If maintaining routes takes more effort than the avoided questions, shrink the scope or stop. If infrequent hiring prevents monthly reuse, consider cohort pricing rather than inventing subscription retention.

## Distribution

Start with manager and new-hire pairs, not mass contact import. A short live demonstration on a fictional stale-owner example can invite interviews; customer private materials must not become marketing footage without separate permission. Slack selected-message setup helps only if the organization allows it. Keep an import-only option for restricted workplaces.

## Potential expansion, conditional on evidence

From one person's approved routes to explicitly shared team context, with organizational access controls, SSO, revocation and a manager-maintained source inventory. Each is future work and requires separate design. Broader relation/project memory can remain useful after the first week; measure continued use instead of assuming an enterprise memory moat.

No verified customers, revenue, retained cohorts, ROI, enterprise approval, funding or PMF are claimed.
